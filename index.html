The Food Security Problem

Optimizing Rice Production (Variety: MARDI MR297)

In Malaysia, securing domestic rice supply is a paramount national food security goal. To optimize yields, agronomists model rice crop growth (dry biomass accumulation) over its typical 120-day cycle.

By calculating the first derivative \(f'(x)\), we find the precise rate of biomass accumulation (\(g/m^2/day\)). This rate reveals critical biological inflection points, indicating exactly when nitrogen top-dressing is necessary (highest growth speed) and when the plant transitions to grain filling, making fertilization wasteful and ecologically damaging.

Mathematical Formulations

Crop Biomass Growth Function \(f(x)\)
$$f(x) = \frac{L}{1 + e^{-k(x - x_0)}}$$

A Logistic Growth Model representing total dry biomass (\(g/m^2\)) at day \(x\).

Biomass Accumulation Rate \(f'(x)\)
$$f'(x) = \frac{L \cdot k \cdot e^{-k(x - x_0)}}{(1 + e^{-k(x - x_0)})^2}$$

The first derivative of \(f(x)\) with respect to time, giving the daily growth rate (\(g/m^2/day\)).

\(x\): Days After Transplant (DAT)
\(L\): Max Carrying Capacity
\(k\): Growth Steepness Coeff.
\(x_0\): Midpoint Inflection (Day)
